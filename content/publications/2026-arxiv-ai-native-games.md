##### Abstract

Generative AI now lets games produce dialogue, quests, characters, images, and worlds at runtime. This paper defines an *AI-native game* as one in which runtime generative AI is essential to the core loop — removing it would collapse or fundamentally change play. Applying this test to 53 public games and prototypes, we propose a two-axis taxonomy of player-facing game type and dominant AI mechanic, and observe that current titles concentrate in language-driven designs such as narrative adventures. We argue that the central design problem is turning open-ended AI output into stable gameplay through mechanical invariants, and close with a roadmap covering controllable generation, multi-agent systems, evaluation, safety, and regulation.
